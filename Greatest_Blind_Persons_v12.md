## Preface

I am a long-term member of the National Federation of the Blind and have participated actively in the American Council of the Blind, and I continue to have friends and colleagues across both organizations regardless of their affiliations. I disclose this because my judgments in this book have inevitably been shaped by those associations, even as I have strived to evaluate individuals on merit above all.

## Introduction

This book showcases blind, or severely visually impaired, persons considered to be among the greatest of all time. Although some may have had partial vision at points in their lives, their noted accomplishments were achieved primarily without the benefit of eyesight.

The goal was to identify the greatest persons in each of 20 fields of endeavor, defined over several iterations so that each category features at least three persons and no individual appears more than once. The result is exactly 100 people across 20 fields. Some individuals appear in a category for which they were not their most famous, in order to maximize the number of persons showcased overall. Research drew on multiple sources involving deliberative selection processes, supplemented by my own judgment and by suggestions from colleagues. Many suggestions were also informed by AI. The web page linked to each person is a launch point for further biographical information; in fact, the availability of such a link has been a factor in deciding whom to include.

Greatness here is not a statement about a person's intrinsic worth. It is an estimate of how influential or accomplished the person has been as a blind individual -- for the common good of society, for the specific progress of the blind, or both. I weigh both the level of skill exhibited and its influence on the advancement of blind people. An accomplishment may be extraordinary in skill and largely unsung, or modest in execution and highly influential; both can qualify.

These judgments are necessarily subjective, and this book makes no claim to be the authoritative word on the matter. The phrase "Among the Greatest" in the subtitle is deliberate: the list is inclusive by design, and the no-duplicates rule is intentional in order to maximize the number of persons featured. Selection has also been shaped by the existence of written, searchable records in English, which favors more recent individuals and those documented in that language.

I used the latest GPT as one research tool among many, and any errors are my responsibility. I welcome factual corrections and differing opinions, and I will update the text as warranted. I do not want perfection to be the enemy of the good.

## Advocacy and Leadership
[Jim Gashel](https://nfb.org/about-us/leadership/board-directors/james-gashel)
(United States, 1946 - present) served for decades as the National Federation of the Blind's chief liaison to Congress and government agencies, shaping major legislation on Social Security benefits, vendor employment, and the rights of blind workers, earning recognition as one of the most effective disability policy advocates in Washington.

[Newel Perry](https://sites.aph.org/hall/inductees/perry/)
(United States, 1873 - 1961) taught mathematics at the California School for the Blind for decades while mentoring the students who would go on to found the organized blind movement in America, making him the most influential forerunner of the National Federation of the Blind.

[Jacobus tenBroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_tenBroek)
(Canada/United States, 1911 - 1968) founded the National Federation of the Blind in 1940 and served as its first president, articulating a philosophy of self-determination that became the intellectual foundation of the organized blind movement in America.

## Arts, Design, and History
[Esref Armagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esref_Armagan)
(Turkey, 1953 - present) has been blind from birth yet taught himself to paint in perspective-accurate, color-rich detail using touch and texture alone, producing work recognized by art critics and neuroscientists alike and challenging fundamental assumptions about the relationship between sight and visual cognition.

[William Artman](https://www.amazon.com/Beauties-Achievements-Blind-William-Artman/dp/1162603437)
(United States, 1825 - 1912) co-authored *Beauties and Achievements of the Blind*, documenting blind contributions across history and disciplines at a time when such accomplishments were widely unknown, helping to build an early evidence base for a more expansive public understanding of blindness.

[Peggy Chong](https://theblindhistorylady.com/)
(United States, 1961 - present), known as "The Blind History Lady," has dedicated her career to researching, writing, and speaking about the history of blind Americans, recovering forgotten stories and building public awareness of a tradition of achievement that mainstream historical records had largely overlooked.

[John Herreshoff](https://www.herreshoff.org/john-brown-herreshoff/)
(United States, 1848 - 1915) designed championship racing yachts that won the America's Cup multiple times after losing his sight in his twenties, working by memory and touch in partnership with his brother Nathanael to produce some of the most technically advanced sailing vessels of the nineteenth century.

[James Wilson](https://loc.gov/loc/lcib/9614/wilson.html)
(United States/Ireland, 1779 - 1845) authored *Biography of the Blind*, one of the earliest systematic attempts to document the lives and achievements of blind individuals throughout history, creating a foundational reference work that helped establish the very concept of a tradition of blind accomplishment.

## Athletics and Sport
[Mike May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_May_%28skier%29)
(United States, 1953 - present) became world-champion blind downhill skier competing against sighted rivals, then co-founded the *Sendero Group* to pioneer GPS navigation technology for blind users, becoming one of the most influential figures in the development of accessible travel aids for the blind.

[Oral O. Miller](https://www.acb.org/memoriam-oral-o-miller-0)
(United States, 1933 - 2023) founded and organized competitive blind athletics programs across multiple sports -- including bowling, wrestling, and dragon boating -- through the American Council of the Blind, establishing infrastructure that gave blind people organized sporting opportunities where none had existed.

[Erik Weihenmayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Weihenmayer)
(United States, 1968 - present) became, in 2001, the first blind person to summit Mount Everest, then completed the "7 Summits" -- the highest peak on each continent, competing alongside sighted mountaineers at the highest levels of the sport.

## Business and Entrepreneurship
[Gordon Gund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Gund)
(United States, 1939 - present) built a career as a sports franchise owner and major philanthropist after losing his sight to retinitis pigmentosa in his thirties, co-founding the Foundation Fighting Blindness to invest in research toward cures for blinding diseases.

[Christine Ha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_H%C3%A0)
(Vietnam/United States, 1979 - present) won the third season of *MasterChef* in 2012 as a blind chef competing against sighted rivals, then opened *The Blind Goat* restaurant in Houston and authored a cookbook, establishing herself as an entrepreneur and public advocate for blind creativity in the culinary world.

[Ted Henter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Henter)
(United States, 1950 - present) co-founded *Henter-Joyce*, later *Freedom Scientific*, and led the development of JAWS (Job Access With Speech), which became the world's most widely used screen reader and opened personal computing to an entire generation of blind users.

[Hans Jørgen Wiberg](https://rarerevolutionmagazine.com/peopleofrare/hans-jorgen-wiberg-of-be-my-eyes/)
(Denmark, 1939 - present) invented and founded *Be My Eyes*, a free app connecting blind users with sighted volunteers and AI-powered assistance worldwide, transforming a simple insight about mutual helpfulness into a global service used by millions.

## Digital Media and Podcasting
[Molly Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molly_Burke)
(Canada/United States, 1994 - present) built a following of millions on YouTube and TikTok with candid, humorous, and informative content about life as a blind woman, becoming one of the most prominent disabled content creators in the world and a public voice for blind visibility and disability pride.

[Tommy Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Edison)
(United States, 1963 - present), known as "The Blind Film Critic," has built a loyal YouTube following by reviewing films from a nonvisual perspective and exploring questions about blind experience, normalizing blindness and fostering empathy across a broad audience.

[Jonathan Mosen](https://mosen.org/bio/)
(New Zealand, 1969 - present) created the *Living Blindfully* podcast and *Mosen Explosion* blog, becoming one of the most trusted and widely followed voices in the global blind technology community, while also working as a broadcaster, author, and accessibility consultant for major technology companies.

## Disability Rights and Law
[Tilly Aston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilly_Aston)
(Australia, 1873 - 1947) became the leading voice for blind Australians of her era, founding and editing publications for the blind, authoring poetry and prose, and organizing the advocacy work that shaped public policy and social attitudes toward blindness in Australia.

[Haben Girma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haben_Girma)
(United States, 1988 - present) became the first DeafBlind graduate of Harvard Law School, was named a White House Champion of Change by President Obama, and secured a landmark ADA ruling that online businesses must be accessible to blind users, becoming one of the most prominent disability rights lawyers of her generation.

[Scott LaBarre](https://nfb.org/celebration-of-life-scott-labarre-program)
(United States, 1968 - 2022) led the advocacy effort that secured United States ratification of the Marrakesh Treaty -- enabling cross-border sharing of accessible books for the print disabled -- while also advancing disability rights within the American Bar Association.

[Marc Maurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Maurer)
(United States, 1951 - present) led the National Federation of the Blind for over two decades, using litigation to compel technology access for the blind and overseeing construction of the organization's landmark city block-spanning headquarters in Baltimore.

[Durward K. McDaniel](https://sites.aph.org/hall/inductees/mcdaniel/)
(United States, 1915 - 1994) was the founding national representative of the American Council of the Blind and devoted decades to the principle that blind people themselves should lead in shaping the policies that govern their lives, helping to establish consumer-led advocacy as the foundation of the modern blind rights movement.

[Otis Stephens](https://news.utk.edu/2016/12/05/memoriam-otis-hammond-stephens-jr/)
(United States, 1936 - 2016) earned the title of Macebearer -- the University of Tennessee's highest faculty award -- as head of the political science department, authored *American Constitutional Law* now in its sixth edition, and was later appointed resident scholar of constitutional law at UT's College of Law, all while blind from birth.

## Education and Literacy
[Louis Braille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Braille)
(France, 1809 - 1852) invented, at the age of fifteen, the tactile six-dot code that bears his name, creating a system adaptable to virtually any language and notation that became the universal standard for blind literacy worldwide and remains in use nearly two centuries later.

[Isabelle Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Grant)
(United States, 1896 - 1977) became the first blind person to teach in the California public school system and earned two Fulbright Fellowships to advance education for blind children in Pakistan and Africa, helping to establish Braille libraries in 65 countries and successfully fighting to remove discriminatory certification requirements for blind teachers in California.

[William A. Hadley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_A._Hadley)
(United States, 1860 - 1941) founded the Hadley School for the Blind in 1920, pioneering correspondence-based distance education that made academic and vocational instruction available to blind adults regardless of location and establishing an institution that continues to serve thousands of students worldwide.

[Robert Irwin](https://sites.aph.org/hall/inductees/irwin/)
(United States, 1883 - 1957) served as executive director of the American Foundation for the Blind and was instrumental in establishing the Talking Book program and expanding the national production and distribution of braille materials, dramatically improving the literacy and cultural access of blind Americans.

[Helen Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller)
(United States, 1880 - 1968) overcame DeafBlindness from infancy to earn a college degree, author multiple books, and become an internationally recognized advocate for the education and inclusion of people with disabilities, making her the most widely known blind person in history.

[Sabriye Tenberken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabriye_Tenberken)
(Germany, 1970 - present) co-founded *Braille Without Borders* and its successor *Kanthari*, developing locally adapted braille systems and training community leaders in multiple countries to establish programs for the blind, proving that nonvisual education and empowerment can take root even in the most resource-limited settings.

## Employment and Rehabilitation
[Francis Joseph Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Joseph_Campbell)
(United States/United Kingdom, 1832 - 1914) co-founded the Royal Normal College for the Blind in London, establishing a rigorous academic and vocational curriculum that trained blind students for skilled employment at a time when blindness was widely treated as a near-total barrier to productive work, earning a knighthood for contributions that set the model for blind rehabilitation in Britain and beyond.

[Kenneth Jernigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Jernigan)
(United States, 1926 - 1998) directed the Iowa Commission for the Blind and turned it into a national model for high-expectation rehabilitation training, then led the National Federation of the Blind for decades as president and intellectual voice, editing the *Braille Monitor* and shaping the movement's culture through hundreds of speeches and writings.

[Fredric K. Schroeder](https://nfb.org/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm99/bm990510.htm)
(Peru/United States, 1957 - present) served as Commissioner of the U.S. Rehabilitation Services Administration, championed quality orientation and mobility instruction, and worked to standardize literary braille, helping to professionalize and elevate blind rehabilitation nationally.

## Exploration and Navigation
[Morris Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Frank)
(United States, 1908 - 1980) co-founded The Seeing Eye in 1929 -- the first guide dog school in the United States -- after traveling to Switzerland to train with Dorothy Eustis's program, transforming the independence and mobility of blind Americans and catalyzing the global guide dog movement.

[James Holman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Holman)
(United Kingdom, 1786 - 1857), known as "the Blind Traveler," circumnavigated the globe and visited every inhabited continent in the nineteenth century using sound, touch, and echolocation to navigate, writing vivid travel accounts that made him one of the most celebrated adventurers of his era and earned the admiration of Charles Darwin.

[John "Blind Jack" Metcalf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Metcalf_%28civil_engineer%29)
(United Kingdom, 1717 - 1810) built a career as a road engineer while blind from early childhood, surveying routes and supervising construction of nearly 180 miles of turnpike road across northern England using a measuring staff and an intuitive command of terrain, becoming one of the first professional road engineers in Britain.

## Government - National and Military
[David Blunkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Blunkett)
(United Kingdom, 1947 - present) rose to become Home Secretary of the United Kingdom, overseeing major policy reforms in education, employment, and criminal justice, and served as one of the most prominent and influential blind politicians in modern democratic history.

[Matthew A. Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_A._Dunn)
(United States, 1886 - 1942) represented Pennsylvania's 23rd congressional district across multiple terms in the U.S. House of Representatives while blind, serving as chairman of the House Census Committee.

[Thomas P. Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_P._Gore)
(United States, 1870 - 1949) became one of Oklahoma's first two U.S. Senators upon statehood in 1907 and served multiple terms, earning a reputation as an independent voice in Congress.

[Kathy Martinez](https://dralegal.org/staff-members/kathy-martinez/)
(United States, 1959 - present) served as Assistant Secretary of Labor for Disability Employment Policy, shaping federal strategy on workforce inclusion while also building a long career as an international advocate for disability rights across Latin America and globally.

[Rami Rabby](https://diplomacy.state.gov/stories/avraham-rabby-how-a-disability-rights-advocate-opened-the-door-for-blind-diplomats/)
(Israel/United States, 1942 - 2007) became the first blind American diplomat to serve as an ambassador in the U.S. Foreign Service, breaking a significant barrier at a time when the State Department had no precedent for placing blind officers in ambassadorial roles.

[David S. Tatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_S._Tatel)
(United States, 1942 - present) served for decades on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit -- one of the nation's most influential federal courts -- authoring significant opinions on civil rights, administrative law, and the First Amendment while progressively losing his sight.

[George V of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V_of_Hanover)
(Germany, 1819 - 1878) governed the Kingdom of Hanover while blind through the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, and though his kingdom was ultimately annexed by Prussia, refused to surrender his claim and continued to assert his royal legitimacy until his death in exile.

[John of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Bohemia)
(Luxembourg/Bohemia, 1296 - 1346) rode into battle at Crécy during the Hundred Years' War despite having been totally blind for years, dying in combat and becoming a legendary symbol of chivalric courage whose motto, "Ich dien" ("I serve"), was adopted by the Prince of Wales.

## Government - State and Local
[Richard Edlund](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm09/bm0902/bm090215.htm)
(United States, 1925 - 2009) served in the Kansas House of Representatives as a blind legislator, using his direct experience of blindness to inform his legislative priorities.

[John Fielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fielding)
(United Kingdom, 1721 - 1780), known as "the Blind Beak of Bow Street," earned a formidable reputation as a London magistrate who could identify criminals by voice alone, and co-founded what became the Bow Street Runners, an early precursor to the modern professional police force.

[Cyrus Habib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Habib)
(United States, 1981 - present) won election as Lieutenant Governor of Washington State in 2016, becoming the first Iranian-American Lieutenant Governor in U.S. history.

[Homer Page](https://www.blind.net/resources/employment/civic-activist-boulder-county-commissioner-homer-page.html)
(United States, 1941 - present) was elected to multiple positions in Boulder, Colorado -- including Deputy Mayor and Chairman of the Board of County Commissioners -- building a long record of civic service at the local level.

[David Paterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Paterson)
(United States, 1954 - present) became the first legally blind governor in United States history and the first African-American governor of New York, serving from 2008 to 2010 after the resignation of Governor Eliot Spitzer and navigating the state through a severe budget crisis.

## Mathematics and Philosophy
[Diodorus Cronus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diodorus_Cronus)
(Greece, 4th century BC) formulated the Master Argument, an influential contribution to the logic of possibility and necessity in ancient Greece that was debated for centuries and anticipated themes in modern modal logic.

[Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler)
(Switzerland, 1707 - 1783) authored more mathematical papers than any other mathematician in history, making foundational contributions to calculus, graph theory, number theory, and mechanics -- much of it dictated to assistants during the years of total blindness that marked the last decade and a half of his career.

[Jacques Lusseyran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Lusseyran)
(France, 1924 - 1971) founded and led a French Resistance network against Nazi occupation as a blind young man, survived imprisonment in Buchenwald, and later became a philosopher and author whose writings on inner light and nonvisual perception continue to be widely read.

[Bernard Morin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Morin)
(China/France, 1931 - 2018) was the first to demonstrate that a sphere can be turned inside out without creasing -- a landmark result in topology -- using the extraordinary tactile spatial imagination he developed after losing his sight at age six to accomplish mathematical visualization that sighted colleagues found difficult.

[Nicholas Saunderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Saunderson)
(United Kingdom, 1682 - 1739) was appointed Lucasian Professor of Mathematics at Cambridge University -- the chair once held by Isaac Newton -- while blind from infancy, teaching effectively with a tactile calculation device of his own invention.

## Music - Instrumental
[José Feliciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Feliciano)
(Puerto Rico/United States, 1945 - present) won multiple Grammy Awards and achieved breakthrough fame with his soulful rendition of "Light My Fire" and the perennial holiday classic "Feliz Navidad," becoming a major figure in bringing Latin guitar and music to English-speaking audiences worldwide.

[Jeff Healey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Healey)
(Canada, 1966 - 2008) developed a unique technique of playing guitar flat across his lap after losing both eyes to retinoblastoma in infancy, achieved international fame with the *Jeff Healey Band*, earned a Grammy nomination, and appeared in the film *Road House*.

[George Shearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Shearing)
(United Kingdom/United States, 1919 - 2011) developed the "locked-hands" chord voicing style that became known worldwide as "The Shearing Sound," recorded hits including "September in the Rain" with his quintet, and was ultimately awarded an OBE and a Kennedy Center Honor for lifetime contributions to jazz.

[Nobuyuki Tsujii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuyuki_Tsujii)
(Japan, 1988 - present) won the Gold Medal and Crystal Award at the Van Cliburn International Piano Competition in 2009 while blind from birth, memorizing and interpreting complex classical repertoire entirely by ear and building an international concert career that has made him one of Japan's most celebrated cultural figures.

[Arthel Lane "Doc" Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doc_Watson)
(United States, 1923 - 2012) won seven Grammy Awards including a Lifetime Achievement Award and is credited with popularizing the flatpicking guitar style that transformed acoustic music, becoming one of the most celebrated blind musicians in American folk and bluegrass history.

[Matthew Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Whitaker_%28pianist%29)
(United States, 2001 - present) emerged as one of the most gifted young jazz pianists and composers of his generation, performing publicly and releasing original work from childhood and collaborating with prominent musicians while blind from birth.

[Tom Wiggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_Tom_Wiggins)
(United States, 1849 - 1908) became the most widely toured American concert performer of the nineteenth century -- enslaved, blind, and autistic -- astonishing audiences with his ability to mimic any sound after a single hearing and to compose and perform sophisticated original piano works, leaving a legacy of extraordinary artistic achievement under conditions of extreme adversity.

## Music - Vocal
[Andrea Bocelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bocelli)
(Italy, 1958 - present) became one of the best-selling recording artists in the history of classical and crossover music, selling over 90 million records worldwide and earning a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

[Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles)
(United States, 1930 - 2004) forged a revolutionary musical style blending jazz, gospel, blues, and country into what became known as soul music -- winning seventeen Grammy Awards -- after teaching himself piano by ear at a school for the blind while growing up in poverty in the segregated American South.

[Fred Lowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Lowery)
(United States, 1909 - 1984) became the most celebrated concert whistler of the twentieth century, performing with major orchestras and on national radio programs and elevating whistling to a recognized musical art form.

[Ronnie Milsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Milsap)
(United States, 1943 - present) recorded 40 number-one country singles, won six Grammy Awards, and was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame, reshaping the sound of mainstream country music with his crossover pop sensibility.

[Diane Schuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Schuur)
(United States, 1953 - present) won back-to-back Grammy Awards for Best Jazz Vocal Performance and rose to prominence after a celebrated appearance at the Monterey Jazz Festival, earning recognition as one of the most gifted jazz singers of her generation.

[Stevie Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Wonder)
(United States, 1950 - present) renegotiated creative control of his music at Motown in the 1970s, released landmark albums including *Innervisions* and *Songs in the Key of Life*, won 25 Grammy Awards plus the Lifetime Achievement Award, and became one of the most celebrated and influential musicians in the history of popular music.

## Radio, TV, and Film
[Ed Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Lucas)

[Gary O'Donoghue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_O%27Donoghue)

[Margaret Pfanstiehl](https://www.acb.org/memoriam-margaret-pfanstiehl)

[Tom Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Sullivan_%28entertainer%29)
(United States, 1947 - present) built a multi-platform career as a singer, actor, television personality, and author, appearing in major film and television productions and writing several books.

[Marilee Talkington](https://nfb.org/images/nfb/publications/fr/fr38/1/fr380109.htm)
(United States, 1966 - present) has performed on Broadway and in major television productions including the *NCIS* series as a legally blind actor, while also advocating for authentic disability representation in an industry that rarely cast disabled performers in prominent roles.

[Ed Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Walker_%28radio_personality%29)
(United States, 1932 - 2015) hosted *The Big Broadcast* on WAMU National Public Radio for decades, becoming a beloved voice in Washington, D.C. and one of the most dedicated custodians of classic radio and old-time entertainment.

[Peter White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_White_%28broadcaster%29)
(United Kingdom, 1947 - present) presented *In Touch*, the long-running BBC Radio 4 program for blind and partially sighted listeners, for decades, becoming one of Britain's most familiar and trusted blind media voices.

## Religion and Spirituality
[Didymus the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didymus_the_Blind)
(Egypt, 313 - 398) served as head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria for decades while blind from early childhood, teaching students who included Jerome and Rufinus and composing extensive biblical commentaries that earned him recognition as one of the most learned theologians of the early Christian church.

[Black Elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Elk)
(United States, 1863 - 1950) was an Oglala Lakota medicine man and holy man whose recounting of his visions and ceremonial knowledge -- despite the devastating cultural destruction his people endured, and despite his own progressive blindness in later life -- produced *Black Elk Speaks*, one of the most important documents of Native American spiritual tradition.

[Prospero Fagnani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospero_Fagnani)
(Italy, 1588 - 1678) produced a monumental multi-volume commentary on canon law that became a standard reference work in ecclesiastical jurisprudence for generations, establishing his reputation as one of the most learned legal minds of seventeenth-century Rome while blind.

[John Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Milton)
(United Kingdom, 1608 - 1674) dictated *Paradise Lost* to scribes and family members after losing his sight, producing what is widely regarded as the greatest epic poem in the English language -- a profound theological exploration of creation, sin, and redemption whose influence on English literature and Christian thought has been immeasurable.

[Surdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surdas)
(India, 1478 - 1583) composed thousands of devotional songs and poems in honor of Lord Krishna in the Braj Bhasha dialect, becoming the most celebrated poet of the Shuddhadvaita school of Vaishnavism and leaving a body of devotional literature that continues to be sung and revered across India five centuries later.

## Science
[Jacob Bolotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Bolotin)
(United States, 1888 - 1924) became the first blind person to receive a medical license in the United States, establishing a successful practice specializing in heart and lung disease and leaving a legacy honored by the National Federation of the Blind through its annual Jacob Bolotin Awards.

[John Gough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gough_%28natural_philosopher%29)
(United Kingdom, 1757 - 1825) pursued systematic scientific inquiry in botany, mechanics, meteorology, and optics while blind from childhood, developing such a refined tactile and auditory sensitivity that John Dalton credited him with sparking his own interest in science.

[Francois Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Huber)
(Switzerland, 1750 - 1831) conducted pioneering research into honeybee behavior after losing virtually all of his sight as a teenager, directing the observations of his wife and an assistant through an innovative hinged hive he designed, and producing *New Observations on Bees* -- a landmark that resolved key controversies about bee reproduction and remained authoritative for over a century.

[Abraham Nemeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Nemeth)
(United States, 1918 - 2013) developed the Nemeth Braille Code for Mathematics and Science Notation, enabling blind students to read and write mathematical and scientific content with full precision for the first time, opening the door to STEM education for generations of learners.

[Geerat Vermeij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geerat_Vermeij)
(Netherlands/United States, 1946 - present) won a MacArthur Fellowship and produced seminal work on the evolutionary arms race between marine predators and prey, developing an extraordinary tactile sensitivity for detecting morphological details in mollusk shells that sighted researchers had overlooked.

[Henry Wedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wedler)
(United States, 1988 - present) earned a Ph.D. in chemistry from UC Davis as a blind scientist, pioneered techniques for nonvisual laboratory work, became an expert in sensory science and wine chemistry, and has dedicated his career to training blind youth in STEM.

## Technology and Engineering
[Chieko Asakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chieko_Asakawa)
(Japan, 1958 - present) developed *Home Page Reader* at IBM, one of the first talking web browsers, making the internet navigable for blind users years before accessibility became a recognized industry concern, and has since continued to pioneer AI-powered navigation assistance for blind people.

[Jennison Asuncion](https://accessibility.day/about/)
(Canada/United States, 1975 - present) co-founded Global Accessibility Awareness Day (GAAD) in 2012 -- now observed by major technology companies worldwide -- and has led accessibility engineering at LinkedIn, earning the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal and the AFB's Stephen Garff Marriott Award for his contributions to digital inclusion.

[Tim Cranmer](https://afb.org/aw/3/1/14959)
(United States, 1925 - 2001) invented the Cranmer Abacus -- a modified abacus adapted for blind users that remains in use today, sold by the American Printing House for the Blind -- and pioneered the first electronic braille printer and early braille translation software, earning the title "Thomas Edison of the blind" from colleagues in the field.

[Michael Curran](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mdcurran/?originalSubdomain=au)
(Australia, 1963 - present) co-founded the NVDA (NonVisual Desktop Access) project, delivering a full-featured, open-source screen reader free of charge in dozens of language translations and fundamentally democratizing access to personal computing for blind users around the world.

[George Kerscher](https://www.afb.org/aw/13/6/15802)
(United States, 1952 - present) pioneered the DAISY (Digital Accessible Information System) standard for digital talking books and led international efforts to establish accessible digital publishing standards, building the infrastructure that now makes millions of titles available to blind and print-disabled readers worldwide.

[T.V. Raman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._V._Raman)
(India/United States, 1965 - present) made pioneering contributions to audio document systems, PDF accessibility, and the nonvisual accessibility of the Android mobile operating system at major research institutions and at Google, influencing accessibility features used by millions of blind users worldwide.

## Writing - Fiction
[Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges)
(Argentina, 1899 - 1986) served as director of the National Library of Argentina while composing essays and poetry of extraordinary intellectual power by dictation and memory after hereditary blindness robbed him of the ability to read, leaving a body of work that has influenced writers, philosophers, and mathematicians worldwide.

[Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)
(Greece, 8th century BC), the legendary blind poet of ancient Greece, is traditionally credited with authoring *The Iliad* and *The Odyssey*, the foundational texts of the Western literary tradition, whose narrative conventions, heroic archetypes, and poetic techniques have shaped literature and storytelling across nearly three thousand years.

[James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce)
(Ireland, 1882 - 1941) completed *Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake* while nearly blind from severe progressive eye disease, dictating portions and relying on assistants to produce works of such formal innovation and psychological depth that they permanently altered the possibilities of the novel.

[Stephen Kuusisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kuusisto)
(United States, 1955 - present) authored *Planet of the Blind*, a memoir widely read in disability studies and general literary circles, published multiple poetry collections, and has taught creative writing at universities, becoming one of the most important literary voices on the experience of blindness.

[Deborah Kent Stein](https://www.nfbnet.org/files/nfb_literature/STEIN.TXT)
(United States, 1948 - present) authored a series of young adult novels featuring blind protagonists at a time when most literary portrayals of blindness relied on sentimental stereotypes by sighted authors, while also editing *Future Reflections*, a periodical for parents of blind children.

## Writing - Nonfiction
[Judy Dixon](https://www.afb.org/blog/judy-dixon)
(United States, 1952 - present) authored numerous guides on applying technology for independence and served as a consumer products specialist at the National Library Service for the Blind, building a reputation as one of the most trusted and authoritative voices on technology access for blind users.

[M. Leona Godin](https://drmlgodin.com/bio-writer-artist-performer-doctor-of-philosophy/)
(United States, 1971 - present) researched and authored *There Plant Eyes: A Personal and Cultural History of Blindness*, weaving autobiography with cultural analysis, history, and science to produce a book widely praised as a definitive humanistic account of blindness, while also working as a playwright and performer.

[Roy Andries de Groot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Andries_de_Groot)
(United Kingdom/United States, 1910 - 1982) built a distinguished career as a food and wine critic and authored several influential books on the subject after losing his sight, producing authoritative gastronomic judgment through taste, smell, and memory alone.

[Deborah Kendrick](https://www.afb.org/node/14103)
(United States, 1950 - present) founded and edited *Tactic*, the first braille magazine devoted to assistive technology, launched a nationally syndicated disability rights column that ran for 30 years, and served as senior features editor of *AccessWorld*, earning the AFB Access Award and other major recognitions across a four-decade career.

## Appendix A: Honorable Mentions

With the many suggestions I received from colleagues, or found through research, it was difficult to limit the count to 100 blind persons, the original objective. Here are 10 more honorable mentions to explore:

[Charlie Crawford](https://www.acb.org/memoriam-charles-h-crawford)
(United States, 1947 - 2020) served as Massachusetts Commissioner for the Blind and then as executive director of the American Council of the Blind, championing consumer empowerment principles in vocational rehabilitation that came to be known as "Charlie's Law."

[Chris Downey](https://www.perkins.org/resource/chris-downey-blind-architect/)
(United States, 1963 - present) returned to practice as an architect after losing his sight to a brain tumor surgery in 2008, developing tactile and auditory design methods and becoming an internationally recognized advocate for sensory-inclusive architecture.

[David Hartman](https://medicine.vtc.vt.edu/past-events/20221011/video-transcript-david-hartman.html)
(United States, 1949 - present) became the first blind graduate of a U.S. medical school to practice as a physician, specializing in psychiatry and advocating for the inclusion of disabled people in the medical professions.

[Fred Gissoni](https://afb.org/aw/7/4/14549)
(United States, 1930 - 2014) became renowned for developing and evaluating practical devices that supported the independence of blind individuals in daily life and work, collaborating with Tim Cranmer on foundational adaptive technology.

[Jim Omvig](https://nfb.org/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm23/bm2301/bm230113.htm)
(United States, 1935 - 2022) directed rehabilitation training programs, authored influential books on the subject, and held senior positions including in the U.S. Social Security Administration, becoming one of the most articulate advocates for high-expectation rehabilitation in the tradition of the National Federation of the Blind.

[Joanne Wilson](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm15/bm1504/bm150409.htm)
(United States, 1946 - present) served as Commissioner of the U.S. Rehabilitation Services Administration, founded the Louisiana Center for the Blind, and co-founded the National Blind Certification Board, raising professional standards across the field of orientation and mobility instruction.

[Marla Runyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marla_Runyan)
(United States, 1969 - present) became the first legally blind athlete to compete in the Olympic Games, finishing eighth in the 1500 meters at the 2000 Sydney Olympics while also earning multiple Paralympic medals, competing across both sighted and Paralympic track and field at the highest levels.

[Joshua Miele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Miele)
(United States, 1969 - present) earned a MacArthur Fellowship for inventing a range of adaptive technologies for blind users -- including tactile maps and accessible signage systems -- and has worked at Amazon to improve the accessibility of consumer technology.

[Leila Holterhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Holterhoff)
(United States, 1886 - 1968) performed as a concert soprano and devoted much of her life to philanthropic causes supporting people with disabilities, using her artistic platform and personal resources to advance opportunities for blind women in the early twentieth century.

[Mike Calvo](https://pneumasolutions.com/about/mike-calvo-bio/)
(United States, 1961 - present) pioneered accessible online services for blind users, founding the System Access Mobile Network and later Pneuma Solutions, earning recognition as one of the most entrepreneurially innovative figures in the blind technology community.

At Amazon, he developed a framework for accessibility auditing, applying automated and manual testing for the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG). His honors include the *Sir Francis Joseph Campbell Award* from the American Library Association.

Jamal resides with his wife and daughter in Seattle, Washington. He does expert consulting on empowerment policy and technology.
