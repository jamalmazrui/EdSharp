## Overview

In light of the critical role Information and Communication Technology ICT has in contemporary society, including in the employment context, in the first
hundred days the HRC Administration will announce an Initiative on Access to ICT. Accessible ICT is one of the most significant challenges facing
Many individuals with significant disabilities.

For the next four years, the HRC Administration will work tirelessly to eliminate these complex barriers to ensure the civil rights of all individuals
with disabilities. To ensure the Federal government is carrying out its responsibilities under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), the Rehabilitation
Act of 1973, as amended, and other civil rights laws, within the first one-hundred days, I will Appoint a Chief Accessible Technology Officer (CATO)
in the White House Office of Management and Budget to Direct and advance my administrations Accessibility Initiatives to ensure Accessible Information
and Communication Technology (ICT). This senior executive will work in collaboration with the Office of Management and Budgets (OMB) Chief Technology
Officer and all Chief Information Officers throughout the Federal government. The CATO will work in partnership with both the public and private sectors
to advance opportunity and innovation to eliminate the digital divide that has faced individuals with disabilities for far too long.  Agency-level CATO positions would also be encouraged throughout the federal government.

In addition, within the first one-hundred days of my administration, I will convene a White House Summit for all of our stakeholders to formulate a National Strategy on the
best way forward. WE must correct once and for all the challenges individuals with disabilities face by eliminating barriers to information and communication
technology. This will ensure opportunity for individuals with disabilities to participate in all aspects of our communities. Together:

We will work to develop and implement a government-wide strategic plan that empowers both the public and private sectors to confront and address these
challenges.

We will work to ensure Federal government leadership, expertise, and resources address emerging technology challenges and opportunities;

We will work in partnership with the private sector to advance access and innovation;

We will work to advance accessible technology civil rights protections; 

We will work to advance accessible technology to ensure employment opportunity; and 

We will work to ensure access and opportunity throughout all aspects of our society.

## Appoint a federal Chief Accessible Technology Officer (CATO), heading up a CATO work group, operating from either OMB or OSTP at the White House.

The CATO will work in partnership with both the public and private sectors to:

1. Improve coordination across federal agencies on accessible ICT. Opportunities include shared testing and testing resources, open reporting of
results, and shared training materials and classes. CATO will also support the development of communities of practice and further professionalization in
the domain of ICT accessibility.

2. Develop processes for improving quality assurance through metrics, product evaluation protocols, and accessibility reporting tools.

3. Support agencies in developing their own accessibility programs and processes.

4. Support innovation in accessibility procurement, such as targeted evaluation of all products and services purchased through GSA schedules or other
expedited procurement channels.

5. Disseminate widely, to multiple audiences, the advantages to and availability of accessibility in mainstream ICT, and the continuing advantages
of assistive technology in many situations. This may require partnering with existing information sources, both public and private, about accessible ICT
and participating in the creation of a plan for expanding their reach and effectiveness.

## Host a White House summit on accessible information and communication technology.

A White House Summit on Accessible Technology Policy will engage stakeholders to draft a National Strategy for technological inclusion via policy and program innovation.  This would be a comprehensive meeting on accessible ICT -- policy across all sectors, environments of use, technologies, and users. The event would focus on policy rather than tech demos.

The agenda should address topics including the following:

* assistive robots

* self-driving cars

* smart homes and cities via the Internet of Things

* augmented reality

# Direct the federal Artificial Intelligence initiative to target assistive apps for cognitive disabilities as one of the goals of the R&D.

The Obama administration has identified Artificial Intelligence as playing a key role in economic development. This is particularly significant for those
with cognitive and intellectual disabilities. Approximately 75% of technology is abandoned by persons with cognitive and intellectual disabilities. either
due to difficulty in use or failure of tools to meet the needs of individuals. AI provides the potential to create zero effort technologies that can enable
many more individuals to live independently, and compete in the professional marketplace. These tools are likely to have wide ranging positive benefits
for all individuals, including both those with disabilities and those without.  For example, virtual personal assistants that communicate via speech in both directions also benefit persons with visual or dexterity limitations -- whether due to biological traits or environmental constraints.

The Initiative on Access to IT should work to incorporate the use of smart tools which use AI and cognitive systems to help support meaningful employment
opportunities for this undeserved group. To achieve this goal the CATO task force will specifically develop a plan to address these needs, partnering with
persons with cognitive and intellectual disabilities, to define a blue print for the design and development of novel technologies that help support cognitive
challenges. This blue print should be completed within the first `100 days. Execution of this set of tools should then be performed by a partnership of
subject matters experts in the field, including individuals with cognitive challenges, business partners, small businesses, and experts in the field of
AI and funded through the office of the CATO.

## As part of the federal Open Government initiative, direct federal agencies to regularly contribute accessibility-measurement data to a public dashboard on the OMB website

This web-based, accessible dashboard would show how agencies -- both individually and collectively -- are doing with respect to consistent performance metrics on Section 508 implementation.  Agencies would be asked to transparently link to a view of their performance data on the OMB dashboard, which would support various ways of sorting and filtering cross-agency data.

## In order to promote the teaching of accessible technology development and design principles, launch a collaboration with the cross-industry initiative called [Teaching Accessibility](http://teachaccess.org/).

To reach the goal of making existing and emerging technology accessible to everyone, awareness and understanding of basic accessibility issues, concepts and best practices must be ubiquitous among developers and designers in the tech sector. Collaboration is necessary among academia, industry and advocates for people with disabilities as modeled by the Teach Access (http://www.teachaccess.org) Project (www.teachaccess.org (http://www.teachaccess.org)).

The new administration should direct the Department of Education as well as the National Science Foundation, the Commerce Department and the Department of Labor to develop grant programs and initiatives to help students in fields such as design, computer sciences and human computer interaction be better prepared to enter the workforce and create future technologies that are truly inclusive. STEM education initiatives should include the teaching of accessibility and universal design principles in the curricula of computer scientists, designers and researchers in undergraduate, graduate, and continuing education.

 Grants should be developed to foster learning about accessibility in higher education through approaches such as internships, challenge grants for research and curriculum development and industry partnerships.
