## The health of what is on disk

Every one of the 137 passes the structural standard: byte order mark, Windows
line endings, frontmatter, exactly one h1, a contents section, categories
alphabetical with Miscellaneous last, articles alphabetical inside every
category, and no title repeated within a category anywhere in the collection.

Four things are still outstanding, and only two of them matter:

- **Thirty guides carry a `[table]` placeholder** where a table was dropped at
  build time. The content is simply gone and only a re-harvest brings it back.
  Amtrak, Android, AndroidStudio, AppleTV, AppleWatch, Auphonic, BARD,
  CookUnity, DoorDash, EqualAccess, GoldWave, Greyhound, Instacart, Instagram,
  Merlin, Netflix, PBS, PrimeVideo, QuorumStudio, Squarespace, TI84Plus,
  Ulysses, Walgreens, Waymo, Wikipedia, Windows, Wix, WordPress, Zoom, Zotero.
  Measured before as a small concentrated problem rather than a broad one; only
  AndroidStudio and Ulysses have it in more than a sixth of their articles.
- **Zotero still holds eight change logs** — "Zotero 1.0 Version History"
  through "Zotero 7 Version History". The removal pass treats "version history"
  as a change log only when it names a whole category, because Dropbox's
  version history is a feature of Dropbox. At article level these slipped
  through, and they are change logs. VoiceVista has one more, "Version history
  since the Soundscape resurrection". Dropbox's and WordPress's are genuine
  feature articles and should stay.
- **Two guides skip a heading level** — Disqus (twice) and VLC (twice), each
  jumping h3 to h5. Four instances in 34,876 articles.

One thing worth saying about my own measuring. My first pass reported **58
guides with their articles out of alphabetical order**, and every one of them
was a false alarm: I compared heading text that still carried its `{#anchor}`
suffix, and a brace sorts after a letter, so "Bedroom" appeared to come after
"Bedroom Suite". The guides were right and the ruler was wrong. That is the
same lesson as the case-sensitive `[TABLE]` search — the measuring tool must
use the same key as the tool that did the work — and it would have sent you
re-running a repair on 58 files that needed nothing.

## Discontinued, with the reason

Twenty-two products were looked at and will not be gathered. They fall into
kinds rather than being one list:

**The site never becomes an article.** A page is served politely and the words
never arrive, because the help centre is a program rather than a document.

- Salesforce — three scripts on your disk, three routes ruled out
- Paramount+ — the same Salesforce Lightning signature

**The publisher ships its questions and withholds its answers.** The answer is
fetched on click, and a page-dumping browser does not click.

- Whole Foods Market
- H-E-B — and it additionally bot-blocks after repeated automated visits

**Access is refused outright.**

- The Wall Street Journal — its own page says "Access to this page has been
  denied", and its 26 article pages each came back exactly 88 words
- Trader Joe's — 403 on both addresses
- Meijer — 403, and my guess that it ran on Oracle RightNow was wrong

**The lists are drawn in the browser and never served.**

- The New York Times — the migration to Helpjuice made the estate less
  reachable than the Zendesk system it replaced
- Aldi — two words at its customer-service address

**Behind a visual challenge that cannot be solved without sight.**

- Firefox — closed after **eight** script versions. Mozilla places its
  challenge in front of the support API as well as the pages. This is the only
  refusal in the collection whose cause is an accessibility barrier, and it is
  worth saying so in those words.

**There is nothing substantial to gather.**

## The tooling gap, which is the largest single finding

**Not one build script and not one repair tool is in what you uploaded.** The
archive holds gathering scripts, two old probes, and the two shrink tools —
nothing else. Missing entirely:

- The repair chain: `fixGuides.py`, `reorderGuides.py`, `dedupeGuides.py`,
  `dropChangeLog.py`, `auditGuides.py`
- `buildZendeskGuide.py`, which serves all ten Zendesk estates
- Every `build<Product>Guide.py`
- The current probes: `probeEstates2.py`, `probeGrocery.py`,
  `probeNewsAndPharmacy.py`
- `getMediaHelp.py` and `getGroceryHelp.py`'s companions

The guides on disk are in their repaired state, so the tools clearly ran — they
simply were not kept. Without them a new harvest cannot be built into a guide,
and nothing can be audited before it ships. **This is worth fixing before any
of the pending runs**, and it costs you nothing but a message: say the word and
I will reissue the whole toolchain in one zip.
